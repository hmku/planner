    Historical data

    Simulations sample one historical year at a time, with replacement, from the loaded history. Each sampled year supplies an S&P 500 total return, a 3-month T-bill return, and CPI inflation.

    Return model

    Portfolio nominal return is the T-bill return plus SPX beta times the S&P 500 excess return over T-bills. It is converted to current dollars by dividing by that year's inflation factor.

    Dynamic beta

    Backward dynamic programming over plan year and wealth, using a zero bucket plus 180 log-spaced buckets from $10,000 to $1 trillion and betas from 0.0 to 1.5 in 0.1 steps. Each node picks the beta with the lowest estimated depletion risk, breaking ties by expected terminal wealth. The beta for a year is chosen before that year's return is drawn.

    Frontier

    Each dynamic run also solves the policy with scenario-calibrated risk penalties and plots expected terminal wealth against run-out risk for each resulting policy.

    Lifestyle builder

    The builder prices a described lifestyle as dated annual expenses in today's dollars. Kid costs follow each kid's age: childcare to 4, school 5–17, college 18–21. Local costs (childcare, school, help, everyday living) are priced for SF/NYC and scaled down for cheaper areas. Every line can be overridden or moved to a manual row. Taxes and above-inflation cost growth are not modeled.

    Cash flows

    Income and expenditures are annual current-dollar flows. Start and end years can be fixed or tied to the plan's current year and year of death. Lifestyle lines use fixed years; one-time costs such as a home purchase are a single-year flow.

    Timing

    Within each year, returns compound continuously and net cash flow arrives continuously. A path is depleted when current-dollar wealth reaches zero.

    Limits

    This is a historical bootstrap, not a forecast. It ignores taxes, account types, fees, allocation beyond SPX beta, spending adjustments, serial correlation between years, and valuation-based expected returns.
